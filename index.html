The setup

Where we find Ira

Ira is twenty-six and has been in Mumbai for eight months. Eight months is also how long it has been since she walked out of a six-year relationship.

She's rebuilding quietly. A day job writing error messages for a money app. A flatmate who keeps dragging her to parties. A therapist who keeps assigning homework she'd rather skip: do one thing outside your comfort zone.

Then, at a rooftop party in Bandra, a man in a pineapple-print shirt walks over to the water tank where she's hiding. Her head says be careful. Her heart has other plans. Across three books and three cities, Ira has to work out which one to trust, and who she becomes when she finally picks.

Pineapple shirts, a silver thumb ring and a dented Gypsy with no doors. He charms everyone, from the security guard to the vada pav man near Dadar, which is exactly what worries her.

A note from the author

Thank you for reading

Three Cities is my first collection, and the first thing I've ever let strangers read. If you made it here, you spent hours of your own time with Ira and her very complicated heart. That means more to me than I can fit into a neat sentence.

I'm still writing, so your thoughts can still shape what comes next. Tell me what worked and what didn't: the scene you reread, the line that felt off, the guy you were rooting for and the one you weren't. Be kind if you can and honest either way. I read every comment.
